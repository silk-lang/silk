A workspace `silk check` or `silk build` on Linux that runs short of memory while members run side by side now ends the newest member instead of stopping it, so its memory goes back at once, and checks or builds that member again later with nothing beside it. A stopped member kept its memory, and the member left running could still run out: on a 16 GB machine two stopped checks held 6 GB while a third grew to 7 GB and was killed.
